Glossary

Surrogacy terms, explained

60 terms you'll meet in clinic letters, contracts and court papers.

ABCDEFGHILMOPRSTUVW

A

Altruistic surrogacy
Surrogacy in which the surrogate receives no payment beyond reimbursement of documented expenses. The only lawful model in Canada, the UK, Australia, India and several other countries.
Amparo
A Mexican constitutional court action sometimes used to secure intended parents' names on a birth certificate.
Anatomy scan
A detailed ultrasound at roughly 18-22 weeks that checks the baby's development.
ART
Assisted reproductive technology: treatments in which eggs or embryos are handled outside the body, such as IVF.

B

Beta hCG test
A blood test, usually 9-10 days after a day-5 embryo transfer, that measures the pregnancy hormone hCG.
Blastocyst
An embryo five or six days after fertilisation; the stage most often transferred or frozen today.
BMI
Body mass index, weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. Clinics usually set an upper limit for gestational surrogates.

C

Carrier agreement
See gestational carrier agreement.
Commercial surrogacy
Surrogacy in which the surrogate receives compensation beyond expenses. Lawful in most US states and a few other countries; a crime in many others.
Compensated surrogacy
Another term for commercial surrogacy, preferred by many US professionals.
Consular Report of Birth Abroad
A US document recording the citizenship of a child born overseas to a US citizen parent.
Cryopreservation
Freezing eggs, sperm or embryos for later use.

D

DNA test
A genetic test sometimes required to prove a parent-child link for citizenship or parentage.
Donor egg
An egg from a donor rather than the intended mother; used in a large share of gestational surrogacy journeys.

E

Embryo transfer
A brief, usually painless procedure in which a thin catheter places an embryo into the uterus.
Endometrium
The lining of the uterus, prepared with medication before an embryo transfer.
Escrow account
An account held by an independent manager that pays surrogate compensation and expenses as milestones occur. Protects both sides.
Estrogen
A hormone given before transfer to build the uterine lining.
Expenses (reimbursement)
Documented costs a surrogate incurs because of the pregnancy, such as travel, maternity clothes and lost wages. In altruistic systems, the only payments allowed.

F

Frozen embryo transfer (FET)
Transfer of a previously frozen and thawed embryo.

G

Gestational age
Pregnancy age measured from two weeks before conception. A day-5 embryo transfer corresponds to 2 weeks and 5 days of gestational age.
Gestational carrier (GC)
A person who carries a pregnancy created with someone else's egg; she has no genetic link to the baby.
Gestational carrier agreement (GCA)
The contract between intended parents and the surrogate covering compensation or expenses, medical decisions, insurance, risks and parentage. Each side should have its own lawyer.
Gestational surrogacy
Surrogacy through IVF in which the surrogate is not genetically related to the child. The form used in almost all modern arrangements.

H

hCG
Human chorionic gonadotropin, the hormone detected by pregnancy tests.

I

ICSI
Intracytoplasmic sperm injection: a single sperm is injected into an egg during IVF.
Independent journey
A surrogacy arranged without an agency, usually with a known surrogate, relying on clinics, lawyers and escrow directly.
Intended parent (IP)
The person or couple who will raise the child and become the legal parent(s).
IVF
In vitro fertilisation: eggs are fertilised in a laboratory and the resulting embryos transferred to a uterus.

L

Lining check
An ultrasound and blood test confirming the uterine lining is ready for transfer.
Lost wages
Income a surrogate loses because of appointments, bed rest or recovery; commonly reimbursed.

M

Matching
The process of introducing intended parents and a surrogate whose expectations, values and logistics align.
Maternal-fetal medicine (MFM)
A specialist in high-risk pregnancies.
Medical screening
Tests of a surrogate's health, uterus and infectious-disease status before she is cleared for transfer.
Mock cycle
A trial run of medications and monitoring to see how the uterine lining responds.
Multiple pregnancy
Twins or more. Most clinics now transfer one embryo to reduce risk.

O

Obstetrician (OB)
The doctor who manages the pregnancy after the fertility clinic releases the surrogate, typically at 8-10 weeks.

P

Parental order
A UK court order, applied for after birth, that transfers legal parenthood from the surrogate to the intended parents.
Parentage order
A court order declaring the intended parents the legal parents. In supportive US states it can be obtained before birth.
PGT-A
Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy: screening embryos for chromosomal abnormalities before transfer.
Post-birth order
A parentage order issued after the baby is born.
Pre-birth order (PBO)
A court order issued before birth naming the intended parents on the birth certificate.
Progesterone
A hormone, often given by injection, that supports early pregnancy after transfer.
Psychological screening
An evaluation, usually by a licensed mental-health professional, of the surrogate (and partner) and often the intended parents.

R

Rematch
Finding a new surrogate if a match ends before or after screening.
Reproductive endocrinologist (RE)
A fertility specialist who manages IVF and embryo transfer.

S

Second-parent adoption
An adoption that adds a non-genetic parent where the birth state requires it.
Single embryo transfer (SET)
Transferring one embryo at a time; the standard of care in most clinics.
Surrogacy agency
A business or non-profit that recruits and screens surrogates, matches them with intended parents and coordinates the journey.
Surrogacy-friendly insurance
A health plan with no exclusion for surrogate pregnancies. Plans that exclude them require separate coverage.
Surrogate
A person who carries a pregnancy for intended parents.

T

Traditional surrogacy
Surrogacy using the surrogate's own egg, so she is genetically related to the child. Legally riskier and uncommon today.
Transfer fee
A payment some contracts make to the surrogate for each embryo transfer attempt.
Trimester
One of three stages of pregnancy: weeks 1-13, 14-27 and 28 to birth.
Two-week wait
The time between embryo transfer and the pregnancy test.

U

Uterine factor infertility
Infertility caused by an absent or non-functioning uterus; a common medical reason for surrogacy.

V

Validated agreement
In Texas and Utah, a surrogacy agreement approved by a court before pregnancy.
Viability
The point after which a baby could survive outside the uterus with medical help.
Vitrification
Ultra-rapid freezing that protects eggs and embryos from ice-crystal damage.

W

Wait list
In altruistic systems, the queue of intended parents hoping to meet a surrogate; often more than a year.
