The 10 stages of a surrogacy journey

  1. Learn and get advice

    Talk to a fertility doctor about whether a gestational carrier is right for you, and to a reproductive lawyer about your home country's rules.

  2. Create or secure embryos

    IVF with your own or donor eggs. Many clinics recommend genetically tested, frozen embryos before matching.

  3. Choose your path and professionals

    Agency, independent or non-profit route; clinic; attorney; escrow company. Use our 30-question checklist.

  4. Match with a surrogate

    Profiles, video calls and an honest conversation about communication, medical decisions and expectations.

  5. Screening

    Medical clearance at your clinic, psychological evaluation and background checks for the surrogate (and often for you).

  6. Legal agreement

    Separate lawyers for each side. Covers compensation or expenses, insurance, medical decisions, risks and parentage.

  7. Medication and embryo transfer

    A few weeks of medication to prepare the uterine lining, then a short transfer procedure. Pregnancy test about 9-10 days later.

  8. Pregnancy

    Fertility-clinic monitoring until about 8-10 weeks, then regular obstetric care. Agree how you will share appointments and news.

  9. Birth

    A hospital plan agreed in advance: who is in the room, who holds the baby, discharge paperwork.

  10. Parentage and going home

    Pre- or post-birth court orders, birth certificate, and for international parents, citizenship and passport.

Five ways to protect your money and your family

Independent escrow

Funds should sit with a licensed escrow company that is separate from the agency, with statements you can see.

Separate lawyers

You and the surrogate each need your own reproductive lawyer, licensed where the surrogate lives.

Insurance review

Have a specialist read the surrogate's health plan for surrogacy exclusions before you match.

Written fee schedule

Every fee, refund and rematch term in writing. Be wary of guarantees and unusually low fixed prices.

Home-country advice

Check parentage and citizenship rules in your own country before transfer, not after birth.

Respect the surrogate

The best journeys are built on trust. Surrogates keep the right to make decisions about their own medical care.
