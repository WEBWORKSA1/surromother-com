"Independent" surrogacy means building your team yourself rather than paying an agency to recruit, screen, match and coordinate. It is most common when intended parents already know their surrogate, often a sister, cousin or close friend. It can save a large fee, but it shifts work and risk onto you.

Side by side

Agency journey Independent journey
Finding a surrogate Agency recruits and pre-screens You already know someone, or find someone yourself
Typical extra cost (US) US$20,000-45,000 agency fee Often US$3,000-15,000 for optional coordination
Who coordinates Agency case manager You, your lawyer and your clinic
Screening Agency arranges; clinic clears Clinic clears; you arrange psychological and background checks
Rematch if things fail Usually included or priced You start again
Best for People without a surrogate; busy parents; international families Known surrogates; experienced parents; altruistic systems

What you must still do on an independent journey

Skipping the agency doesn't mean skipping the safeguards. You will still need:

  1. A fertility clinic experienced with gestational carriers, which will run medical screening.
  2. A licensed mental-health professional to evaluate and counsel the surrogate and her partner, and usually to meet with you. Many clinics require it.
  3. Two separate lawyers, one for each side, licensed where the surrogate lives. You pay for both.
  4. An escrow or trust arrangement for compensation or reimbursements, even with family.
  5. An insurance review of the surrogate's health plan, plus life cover.
  6. A written agreement signed before any medication.

Some attorneys and coordination services offer "independent journey" packages that handle scheduling, payments and paperwork for a modest fee. That can be money well spent.

Known surrogates: special considerations

Family and friendship make independent surrogacy possible, and they also make it delicate.

  • Talk about the hard topics early. Prenatal testing, termination, selective reduction, how many transfers, contact after the birth, and what each person will be called.
  • Use counselling properly. A counsellor can help everyone say what they really think while things are calm.
  • Respect the surrogate's autonomy. She keeps the right to make decisions about her own body and medical care, however close you are.
  • Plan for family dynamics. How will other relatives be involved? Who will be at the birth?
  • Don't skip compensation or reimbursement terms. Even in altruistic systems, documented expenses should be repaid promptly and transparently.

Independent journeys in altruistic countries

In Canada, businesses cannot lawfully charge to arrange a surrogate's services, so most journeys involve a surrogate found through community or personal connections, with consultants offering support services rather than matching. In the UK, non-profit organisations such as Surrogacy UK and Brilliant Beginnings provide structured routes based on relationships built before conception. In Australia, every state requires counselling and legal advice before treatment, and a court parentage order afterwards.

Which should you choose?

Choose an agency or non-profit programme if you don't have a surrogate, want structured screening and a rematch safety net, or are coordinating from another country. Choose independent if you have a known surrogate, the time and confidence to manage the process, and a strong lawyer and clinic.
