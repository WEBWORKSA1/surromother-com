Surrogacy laws by US state

There is no federal surrogacy law, so the state where the surrogate lives and gives birth usually decides how parentage works. Here is a simplified, four-level summary of all 50 states and DC.

Supportive (15)

Statute or settled practice supports gestational surrogacy for all family types; parentage orders (often pre-birth) are broadly available.

Permitted with conditions (30)

Gestational surrogacy is practised and parentage can be secured, but results may depend on marital status, residency, genetic link or the county court.

Proceed with caution (5)

Surrogacy happens, but statutes void or limit contracts or court outcomes are inconsistent. Specialist counsel is essential.

Restricted (1)

Statute restricts compensated surrogacy or who may use it.

Foreign intended parents: 2026 changes

On 30 June 2026 the Supreme Court upheld birthright citizenship in Trump v. Barbara. On 6 August 2026 the White House issued executive orders that seek to deny federal recognition of citizenship in some commercial-surrogacy births where neither parent is a US citizen, and to curb "birth tourism". The orders were challenged in court and agency guidance was pending at our last review.

Separately, Florida's FIRE Act (from 1 July 2026) bars any party to a Florida surrogacy agreement who is a citizen or resident of seven designated countries. Non-US parents should get US immigration advice before transfer.

Three questions for any state

  1. Can we get a pre-birth order naming both of us, whatever our marital status?
  2. Does the birth hospital's county court follow the state's usual practice?
  3. Does a non-genetic parent need a second-parent adoption afterwards?
